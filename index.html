FAQ

Questions, answered.

Who is it for?

Female executives only: your responsibilities have grown, you're succeeding at work, and energy, stress, relationships, routines or life outside work are getting harder to sustain. You may already have providers, but no clear overall plan.

How do you vet providers?

Every provider we introduce meets four criteria:

  • Licensed in their profession
  • At least 5 years in practice
  • Has helped more than 250 clients
  • Published, or featured in the media

All of them see you virtually, on your own time.

What does $699 include?

A 90-minute Life Assessment by video, your written 360° Life Report within 72 hours, and within 7 days, introductions and first appointments with vetted, virtual providers matched to your needs. $699 CAD + HST.

What happens during the assessment?

A structured, one-to-one video conversation with Karim Atassi, MD. You talk through what you want to achieve, what feels hard to sustain, the support you already have and what you've tried, and all seven areas of your life. You choose what to share.

What happens after the report?

Within 7 days, Karim Atassi, MD introduces you to your own personal, licensed virtual providers, matched to your exact pain points, and arranges your first appointments. You decide who to see.

What if I already have providers?

Keep them. They can stay part of your plan, and your plan includes questions you may want to bring to them. New providers are suggested only where there's a gap.

Do providers cost extra?

Yes. Provider fees are separate from the $699 and paid directly to each provider. Appointment timing depends on each provider's availability.

Are appointments virtual or in person?

Virtual. Your assessment is by video, and every provider we introduce sees you virtually, on your own time.

Is ongoing support included?

No. The offer is your assessment, your plan and your introductions. It doesn't include ongoing support or coordination between providers.

Is this medical care or therapy?

This engagement provides coaching, planning, and provider matching. It does not include medical diagnosis, treatment, or prescribing. Continue working with your healthcare providers for clinical care. Karim Atassi, MD is not acting as your doctor here, and no doctor–patient relationship is created.

Is the SE7EN fee covered by benefits?

The SE7EN fee isn't covered by OHIP, and we can't promise any reimbursement. We choose providers with your benefits in mind, so you can use the coverage you already have; check the details with your insurer.

How is information handled?

What you share is used to prepare your plan. .

VP Business Development, 35, Toronto

Assessment: 90-minute Life Assessment with Karim Atassi, MD, by video. Plan delivered within 72 hours.

1. Goal

Stated goal: “Get my energy and focus back without stepping down.”

Context: Promoted 8 months ago; her team doubled. She is meeting her targets, but describes an afternoon crash most days, poor sleep and weight changes since the promotion.

Constraints: 55–60-hour weeks, travel one week a month. She will not step back from her role.

Her own markers of progress at 90 days:

  • Clear-headed through most afternoons
  • 7 hours of sleep on most nights
  • Training 3 times a week
  • Laptop closed by 9 p.m. on weeknights
  • One standing commitment a week outside work

2. Observations

How to read this: “She reports” is what she told us. “Observed” is what Karim Atassi, MD noticed in the conversation. Self-ratings are her own, from 1 to 10, used to guide discussion: not a medical finding or a validated test.

3. Possible contributing patterns

  1. 01 Short sleep, missed meals, and demanding afternoon meetings may be contributing to her afternoon difficulties.

    She reports
    Her hardest decisions fall between 1 and 4 p.m., often without lunch, on her third coffee, after about 5 h 40 min of sleep.
    Worth exploring
    Whether moving demanding work earlier, protecting lunch and an earlier caffeine cut-off change how her afternoons feel.

  2. 02 Late-evening work may be cutting into her sleep.

    She reports
    Home alone most weeknights, she reopens her laptop after dinner and often works past midnight.
    Worth exploring
    Whether a set finish time and one regular commitment outside work protect her sleep.

  3. 03 Changes in workload, routines, eating, and activity are areas to explore.

    She reports
    Training dropped from 4 sessions a week to 1 after her promotion; food delivery most weeknights.
    Worth exploring
    Which routines are realistic in her current calendar, including travel weeks.

4. Priorities for the next 90 days

  1. Protect her best hours for her hardest work.
  2. Create a firm end to the workday.
  3. Rebuild a training and meal routine that fits travel weeks.

5. Action plan

  • Move high-stakes meetings to 9–11 a.m. where she controls her calendar; block a 30-minute lunch.
  • Laptop closed by 9 p.m. on weeknights; one standing weekly plan with people she chooses.
  • Book three 45-minute training slots a week, with a hotel-gym version for travel weeks.
  • Book an appointment with her family doctor to discuss her fatigue, sleep and weight changes.
